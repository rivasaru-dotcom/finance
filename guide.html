THE JOURNEY

Seven steps to your first home.

Use this as a practical roadmap. You do not need to know everything before you begin.

01

Know your budget

Start with what you can comfortably afford rather than only looking at the maximum a lender may allow.

See the numbers
02

Build your deposit

Understand how much you have saved and what cash you may need beyond the deposit.

03

Allow for buying costs

Consider government charges, conveyancing, inspections, lender costs and other purchase expenses.

04

Understand pre-approval

A loan assessment can help you understand your borrowing position before you shop seriously.

05

Find the right property

Think about location, property condition, ongoing costs and whether the home fits your longer-term plans.

06

Make an offer

Before committing, understand the contract, conditions, deadlines and professional checks you may need.

07

Settlement & keys

Once the transaction completes, your lender and conveyancer help coordinate the final steps to settlement.

BEFORE YOU BUY

Questions worth asking yourself.

Can I comfortably afford the repayments—not just qualify for them?

Would my budget still work if interest rates or household costs changed?

How much cash will I have left after settlement?

Have I considered the property's ongoing costs and condition?

GUIDE FAQ

Before you rely on the plan.

There is no single deposit amount that applies to every buyer. The amount you need depends on the property, lender, loan structure, your circumstances and other purchase costs. Use the calculator as a starting point and confirm your position with a qualified professional.

Pre-approval is an indication from a lender about your potential borrowing position, subject to the lender's assessment, conditions and final approval. It can help you understand a realistic price range before you shop.

Depending on the purchase, costs can include applicable government charges, conveyancing, inspections, registration, lender fees and moving or setup costs. The exact amounts depend on your circumstances and the property.

No calculator should be treated as a final approval or quote. Results are estimates and actual repayments, fees, government charges and eligibility can vary. Confirm important figures with the relevant lender, government authority or qualified professional.
